# Cole Hook Patterns

Hook patterns and frameworks for opening content. Source: Cole Gordon pattern library.

**Platforms:** LinkedIn, Twitter, Email
**Description:** 7 proven hook formulas that consistently stop scroll and drive engagement

---

## Hook Types

### Bold Outcome Hook

**Format:** I [specific action] and [surprising result]

**When to use:** When you have a counterintuitive result to share

**Engagement multiplier:** 2.5x baseline

**Examples:**
- I wrote 100 LinkedIn posts in 30 days and got 50K followers.
- I deleted my calendar and 3x'd my productivity.
- I quit my $200K job to write full-time. Best decision ever.

---

### Contrarian Question

**Format:** What if [conventional wisdom] is wrong?

**When to use:** Challenging industry assumptions

**Engagement multiplier:** 2.1x baseline

**Examples:**
- What if posting daily is killing your growth?
- What if you don't need 10K followers to make $100K?
- What if the best writers don't write every day?

---

### Mistake Admission

**Format:** I [mistake] for [time]. Here's what I learned:

**When to use:** Teaching through vulnerability

**Engagement multiplier:** 2.8x baseline

**Examples:**
- I ghostwrote for free for 6 months. Biggest mistake of my career.
- I built the wrong product for 2 years. Here's what I'd do differently:
- I ignored my email list for 3 years. Cost me $500K.

---

### Pattern Interrupt

**Format:** Everyone does X. I do Y instead.

**When to use:** Positioning yourself as different/better

**Engagement multiplier:** 2.3x baseline

**Examples:**
- Everyone focuses on growth. I focus on retention.
- Most creators batch content. I write in real-time.
- People say 'be consistent.' I say 'be strategic.'

---

### Urgent Observation

**Format:** In [timeframe], [trend] will [change]. Here's how to prepare:

**When to use:** Creating urgency around industry shifts

**Engagement multiplier:** 2.2x baseline

**Examples:**

- This pricing model is dying. Switch before it's too late.

## Hook Principles

- Specificity beats vagueness (use numbers, names, timef rames)
- Stakes matter (money, time, reputation)
- Curiosity gap (tease what's coming without giving it away)
- First 8 words determine if people keep reading
- Personal > theoretical (your story > general advice)

---

## Strategic Insights

- Bold Outcome Hooks work best on LinkedIn (professional results)
- Contrarian Questions perform well on Twitter (debate culture)
- Specific Number Hooks are universal (concrete = credible)
- Mistake Admission builds trust faster than success stories
- Pattern Interrupt positions you as thought leader
- First sentence should be <15 words for mobile readability
- Test hooks with GPTZero - aim for 80%+ human score

---

## Testing Framework

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| Step 1 | Write 3 variations of your hook using different patterns |
| Step 2 | Pick the one with most specificity + stakes |
| Step 3 | Cut it down by 30% (remove fluff) |
| Step 4 | Read it out loud (if it sounds unnatural, rewrite) |
| Step 5 | Check: Does it create curiosity without clickbait? |

---

## Common Mistakes

- Making hook too long (>20 words)
- Being vague ('I learned something important about business')
- No stakes ('I tried a thing and it worked okay')
- Giving away the punchline in the hook
- Using jargon or corporate speak

---

## Pro Tips

- Use power words: 'killed', 'destroyed', '10x'd', 'obliterated'
- Add time pressure: 'in 30 days', 'overnight', 'by next week'
- Include a number: '7 ways', '$100K', '3 mistakes'
- Name drop when relevant: 'Stripe uses this', 'from Y Combinator'
- Break grammar rules if it sounds better
