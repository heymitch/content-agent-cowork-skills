# Dickie Bush Listicle Formula

Listicle frameworks for structured content. Source: Dickie Bush pattern library.

## Overview

- **Creator:** Dickie Bush
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn, Twitter
- **Description:** High-engagement numbered list posts with strong hooks and skimmable insights

## Structure

### Hook

- **Format:** Number + Bold claim (e.g., '7 lessons from $5M in revenue:')
- **Character count:** 40–120

**Examples:**
- 10 lessons from writing 10,000 tweets:
- 7 frameworks that 10x'd my productivity:
- 5 mistakes I made building my first company:

### List Items

- **Count:** 5–10
- **Format:** Bold insight + 1-2 sentence explanation

**Guidelines:**
- Each item should be skimmable in 3 seconds
- Use specific numbers and outcomes
- Vary sentence structure (short, medium, long)
- Include personal anecdotes when relevant

### CTA

- **Format:** Question that invites sharing or personal experience

**Examples:**
- Which one resonates with you?
- What would you add to this list?
- Have you experienced this?

## Strategic Insights

- Lists with 7-10 items perform 40% better than 3-5 items (more perceived value)
- Hook must include NUMBER + OUTCOME to stop scroll
- Each list item = one tweet's worth of content (can be repurposed)
- Odd numbers (7, 9) feel more authentic than round numbers (5, 10)
- Personal stakes in hook ("$5M", "10 years") increase credibility

## When to Use

- Teaching multiple related concepts
- Sharing lessons from experience
- Breaking down complex topic into digestible parts
- Testing audience interest in a topic (outline as content)
- Repurposing long-form content into social posts

## Common Mistakes

- Making list items too long (should be 1-2 sentences max)
- Using vague insights ('be authentic', 'work hard')
- Not numbering items clearly (use 1., 2., 3. or emojis)
- Weak hook that doesn't promise specific value
- No CTA at end (kills engagement)

## Pro Tips

- Front-load best insights (item #1 and #2 should be strongest)
- Use white space aggressively (short paragraphs = more readable)
- Include ONE contrarian take in the list
- Add time context ('in 2024', 'last year') for relevance
- Reference specific tools/people/numbers for credibility
