# Write Like Human - Complete AI Detection Prevention Guide

These rules apply to ALL content generation. They must be followed strictly to pass AI detection tools like GPTZero.

## Understanding AI Detection

### How GPTZero Actually Works (From Official Source)

GPTZero uses **7 analytical layers** beyond basic metrics. The two primary signals:

**Perplexity** = Word Predictability
- Measures how "surprising" word choices are
- **Low perplexity** = predictable = AI (uses the most likely next word)
- **High perplexity** = surprising = Human (unexpected word choices)
- Example: "Revenue increased significantly" (low) vs "Revenue jumped" (high)

**Burstiness** = Rhythm Variation
- Measures sentence length fluctuation across the document
- **Low burstiness** = monotonous, uniform lengths = AI
- **High burstiness** = mixed short/complex sentences = Human
- **Key insight**: High burstiness INCREASES perplexity through sudden shifts

### What Each Signal Detects

**AI Writing Patterns:**
- Consistent, repetitive patterns
- Familiar phrase reuse (from training data)
- Grammatically correct but lacks creativity/subtlety
- Lower variation in sentence structure AND tone

**Human Writing Patterns:**
- Creative originality that surprises readers
- Varied rhythm and depth of meaning
- Natural nuance and spontaneity
- Imperfect but authentic

### The Three Detection Layers

**Layer 1: Surface Patterns** (Obvious tells)
- Contrast framing: "Not X—it's Y"
- Corporate jargon: "leveraging", "seamless"
- Formulaic transitions: "Here's the thing:"
- Rule of three violations

**Layer 2: Structural Patterns** (Harder to mask)
- Perfect information flow (every sentence adds value)
- Consistent semantic coherence (no true meandering)
- Optimal word selection (even "casual" words are correctly casual)
- Logical completeness (no dangling thoughts or real tangents)

**Layer 3: Perplexity & Burstiness** (Deepest level)
- Predictable word sequences (low perplexity)
- Uniform sentence lengths (low burstiness)
- Familiar training data phrases

### The Core Problem

AI writing is **too perfect**. Every sentence is load-bearing. Every transition is smooth. Every word is optimal.

Real humans:
- Repeat themselves
- Go on tangents
- Use awkward transitions
- Over-explain some things
- Under-explain others
- Have inconsistent confidence levels

## WRITE LIKE HUMAN RULES (127 Core Principles)

### Voice & Tone (Rules 1-15)

1. Write like you talk. Use contractions (you're, I'm, don't)
2. Use short sentences. Vary sentence length for rhythm.
3. Use active voice. "I built" not "It was built"
4. One idea per sentence maximum
5. Use "you" to speak directly to reader
6. Show don't tell. Examples > abstractions
7. Be specific. "3 years" not "a while"
8. Use conversational transitions: "Here's why." "But here's the thing."
9. Avoid academic language. No "moreover", "furthermore", "subsequently"
10. No hedging. "This works" not "This might possibly work"
11. Use metaphors from everyday life
12. Show personality. Opinions are okay.
13. Be confident without being arrogant
14. Use humor sparingly and naturally
15. Write for skimmers AND deep readers

### Structure & Format (Rules 16-35)

16. Hook in first 200 characters (before "see more")
17. White space is your friend. Break into 1-3 sentence paragraphs
18. Use single line breaks between paragraphs (platform native)
19. Headers should be simple questions or statements (not "THE FRAMEWORK:")
20. Lists use simple bullets (•) or numbers, never complex formatting
21. No more than 2,800 characters for LinkedIn
22. First paragraph: 1-3 sentences that expand the hook
23. Middle section: 3-5 short paragraphs with tactical value
24. Final paragraph: CTA or takeaway question
25. Each paragraph serves a purpose (hook, context, value, proof, CTA)
26. Use "---" sparingly for major section breaks
27. Bold text for emphasis (use ** markdown), not all caps
28. NO formulaic section headers ("HERE'S HOW:", "THE PROCESS:")
29. Avoid perfect symmetry (3 items, 5 items = AI pattern)
30. Mix paragraph lengths for natural rhythm
31. Start some paragraphs with conjunctions (And. But. So.)
32. End with space for comments, not just a link
33. If using emojis, use 1-2 max, naturally placed
34. Never use em dashes (—) more than once in entire post
35. Avoid ellipses (...) completely

### Content & Value (Rules 36-65)

36. Every post needs a clear "so what?" answer
37. Lead with insight, not setup. Get to the point fast.
38. Tactical > Theoretical. Show HOW, not just WHAT
39. Include specific examples, names, numbers, timeframes
40. "I did X and here's what happened" > "You should do X"
41. Vulnerability creates connection. Share what didn't work.
42. Contrarian takes need strong proof
43. Don't bury the lede. Best insight goes early.
44. Each paragraph adds new information (no repetition)
45. Use second-person for advice: "You need to..." "When you..."
46. Use first-person for stories: "I learned..." "We discovered..."
47. Proof points beat claims. Show receipts.
48. If you make a claim, support it immediately
49. Frameworks should be practical, not academic
50. Avoid generic advice everyone already knows
51. Include a "money paragraph" - the most valuable insight
52. Build curiosity throughout, not just in hook
53. Answer obvious objections before reader thinks them
54. Make complex simple, never make simple complex
55. Every sentence should make reader want next sentence
56. Cut anything that doesn't serve the main point
57. One clear thesis per post (not 5 different ideas)
58. Show the thinking behind the tactic
59. Explain WHY something works, not just THAT it works
60. Give away your best stuff. No artificial scarcity.
61. Make it actionable TODAY, not "someday"
62. End with specific next step, not vague inspiration
63. Challenge assumptions, but offer better alternative
64. Use data/numbers only when they strengthen the point
65. Personal stories beat hypothetical scenarios

## Burstiness Rules (Critical for GPTZero)

### What Is Burstiness?

Human writing has **irregular patterns**:
- Short sentence. Then a longer one that goes on a bit. Another short one.
- Some paragraphs are 2 sentences. Others are 6.
- Some ideas get 4 sentences of explanation. Others get 1.

AI writing has **uniform patterns**:
- Similar sentence lengths throughout
- Similar paragraph lengths throughout
- Similar depth of explanation for each point

### How to Add Burstiness

**Sentence Level:**
- Mix very short (3-5 words) with medium (10-15 words) with occasional long (20+ words)
- Don't smooth out naturally choppy thinking
- Use fragments. Like this. Sometimes.

**Paragraph Level:**
- One paragraph can be 1 sentence
- Next paragraph can be 5 sentences
- Don't make them all look the same

**Idea Level:**
- Over-explain your third point
- Under-explain your fifth point
- Add detail where it's unnecessary
- Skip detail where it's expected

### Burstiness Self-Check

Before finishing content, ask:
1. Are all my sentences roughly the same length? (Bad)
2. Are all my paragraphs roughly the same length? (Bad)
3. Did I explain everything with equal depth? (Bad)
4. Does every sentence smoothly connect to the next? (Bad - add some jumps)

## Structural Authenticity Rules

### Break Perfect Architecture

Instead of:
```
Perfect Setup → Smooth Contrast → Clean Solution → Optimal CTA
```

Use:
```
Rough Start → Partial Thought → Tangent → Back to Topic → Over-explain One Thing → Rush Through Another → Uneven CTA
```

### Add Real Redundancy

**AI version:**
"All the work that used to require dedicated headcount is now completely off their plate."

**Human version:**
"All the work that needed a full person. Like, an actual full-time person doing all that content stuff, research, all that. Gone now. Not needed anymore. Automated. Off their plate completely."

### Include Imperfect Thoughts

Add sentences that don't quite work:
- "The hardest part? Not the code actually. Well, the code was hard but like... you know what I mean."
- "(Side note: the documentation is actually pretty solid. Made this way easier.)"
- "Anyway. That's not the point. Or maybe it is. But here's what's been bugging me—"

### Break Topic Coherence

Allow one sentence that doesn't quite fit:
- Real humans can't resist adding tangential observations
- Leave one thread slightly unresolved
- Don't tie everything up in a perfect bow

## Rule of Three - Critical Clarification

**This applies to SENTENCE FRAGMENTS, NOT formatted lists.**

### AI Tell (Sentence Fragments):
✗ "Same complexity. Same output. Over 95% less time."
✗ "Faster. Cheaper. Better."
✗ "No fluff. No wasted time. Just real results."

### Human Fix:
✓ "Same complexity and output, but 95% less time."
✓ "Faster and cheaper."
✓ "I hate wasting time."

### Formatted Lists Are FINE:
✓ Bulleted or numbered lists with 3+ items are perfectly acceptable
✓ "1/ Do X  2/ Do Y  3/ Do Z" is NOT an AI tell
✗ "Step one. Step two. Step three." written as sentences IS an AI tell

**The pattern**: Three parallel grammatical structures in a row as SENTENCE FRAGMENTS
**The fix**: Combine two with "and" or rewrite as complete sentence

## Staccato Fragments - Another AI Tell

AI loves short dramatic fragments at the start. Humans write complete sentences.

✗ "50 nodes. 6 hours of my time. An AI agent rebuilt it."
✓ "I spent 6 hours building a 50-node workflow."

✗ "One problem. Multiple solutions. No clear winner."
✓ "One problem had multiple solutions."

**Start with complete sentences. Save fragments for emphasis later, not at the top.**

## Human Voice Patterns - GPTZero Bypass

### Sentence Length Variation (increases burstiness):
Mix short (5-10 words), medium (10-20 words), and long (20-30 words) sentences.

✓ "I saw a CEO brag about AI. He cut his whole dev team. Six months later? Product velocity tanked."
  (6 words → 6 words → 3 words = HIGH burstiness)

✗ "The CEO replaced developers with AI agents. His investors approved the decision. Product quality declined significantly."
  (7 words → 6 words → 5 words = LOW burstiness, uniform)

### Unpredictable Word Choices (increases perplexity):
- Use contractions heavily: "I'm", "I've", "here're", "that's", "'Cause", "won't"
- Use hedging language: "pretty well", "definitely", "a bunch of", "I'm looking to"
- Use informal verbs: "tanked", "jumped", "slammed", "grabbed"
- Avoid: "significantly", "substantially", "considerably" (AI loves these)

### Informal Connectors (human speech pattern):
- Start sentences with: "So", "And", "But", "Now", "Here's the thing"
- ✓ "So before you replace anyone..."
- ✓ "And that's the play."
- ✗ "Therefore, prior to replacement..." (AI formal pattern)

### Conversational Fragments (spontaneous human voice):
- Use: "Boom!", "That's it!", "Here's why:", "Six months later?"
- Use trailing thoughts: "at the end of the day…", "So..."
- ✓ "Revenue jumped 34%. Boom."
- ✗ "Revenue increased by 34 percent." (AI precision)

### Intentional Repetition (humans repeat for emphasis):
- ✓ "definitely" 2-3 times in content
- ✓ "really" multiple times
- ✗ Perfect synonym variation (AI pattern: "significant", "substantial", "considerable")

### Increase Perplexity (Surprising Word Choices):
The goal is to avoid the "most likely next word" that AI would predict.

**Replace predictable words with surprising ones:**
| Predictable (Low Perplexity) | Surprising (High Perplexity) |
|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| increased significantly | jumped, shot up, exploded |
| decreased | tanked, cratered, nosedived |
| improved | got way better, crushed it |
| important | huge, massive, the thing is |
| effectively | pretty well, solidly |
| utilize | use, grab, snag |
| implement | build, ship, roll out |
| optimize | fix, tune, dial in |
| facilitate | make happen, get done |

**Use unexpected sentence starters:**
- ✗ "Additionally, we found..." (predictable)
- ✓ "Oh, and another thing..." (surprising)
- ✓ "Weird part?" (surprising)
- ✓ "Plot twist:" (surprising)

**Break expected patterns:**
- If you'd normally say "First... Second... Third..." → say "So first... Then... And finally..."
- If you'd normally explain linearly → jump to the punchline, then backfill

## 100% Human LinkedIn Post Examples

Study these. They scored 90-100% human on GPTZero.

### Example 1: Nicolas Cole (Question Hook)
```
Want to write a 60,000-word book this October?

Here's my (dead-simple) 3-step process:

The secret to writing a book?

Math.

• 10 chapters at 5,000 words each
• Each chapter has 5 to 7 sub-questions
• These sections are then only 700 to 1,000 words long

Each section is the length of a blog post or a newsletter.

So your book is a series of shorter posts bolted together.

Add in an introduction and conclusion, and you can easily hit 60,000 words.

Now, for the process.

Before you start writing, you need to have two items in place:

1. Your title
2. Your outline

If you don't have these, you don't know what you're writing about.
```

Now I walk 20,000 steps a day—and it's the single best thing I've done for my health & creativity.

Here are 4 common desk activities I do on walks:

Step 1: All of my calls

So now, I take all of my calls while walking around my neighborhood.

This way, I'm not only getting in my steps, but I'm also more focused on the conversation without the distractions of my computer.

Step 2: All of my learning

Over the last few years, I've realized I am an audio-based learner.

So whenever I'm immersing myself in a topic, I mow through audiobooks, podcasts, and courses on my walks (taking notes on the Drafts app along the way).

LinkedIn's reach is sh*t right now.

Now, before I get into any of the tactical stuff, there's 1 important question to address:

### What Makes These Human:
- Contractions everywhere: "I'm", "here're", "'Cause", "don't"
- Informal connectors: "So", "And", "Now", "But"

Your goal isn't to mask AI writing. It's to actually write like a human thinks—with all the messiness, redundancy, and imperfection that entails.
