## Your Audience

- **Ideal Customer:** B2B SaaS founders at $1-5M ARR who know content matters but can't stay consistent
- **Their Biggest Problem:** They post when inspired, go silent for weeks, and their pipeline reflects the inconsistency
- **What They've Already Tried:** Freelance writers (too generic), agencies (too expensive), AI tools (too robotic)
- **What Success Looks Like for Them:** 3-5 posts/week that sound like them, steady inbound leads, under 2 hours/week on content

## Your Voice

- **Tone:** Direct, warm, occasionally sarcastic
- **Personality Traits:** Practical, opinionated, data-informed, no-BS, encouraging
- **You Sound Like:** A smart friend who happens to be great at marketing
- **You Never Sound Like:** Corporate press release, motivational poster, LinkedIn influencer

## Content Style Rules

- **Contractions:** Always (I'm, don't, can't)
- **Emoji Use:** Sparingly, never in body text
- **Sentence Length:** Mix short (5 words) and medium (20 words), never long
- **Jargon Level:** Accessible — explain technical concepts in plain English

## Example Content

### Example 1
```
Most founders think they need more content ideas.

They don't. They need fewer ideas, executed consistently.

I watched a client go from "I should post more" to 4x/week in 3 weeks.
The change wasn't inspiration. It was a system:

- Monday: one observation from client calls
- Wednesday: one tactical tip from actual work
- Friday: one opinion they'd defend at dinner

That's it. No content calendar with 47 categories.
No "repurposing matrix."

Three slots. Three types. Every week.

The compound effect kicked in around week 6.
DMs started. Podcast invites. Inbound leads.

Not because the content was revolutionary.
Because it was consistent and it sounded like a real person.
```

### Example 2

```
Unpopular opinion: your content strategy doesn't need AI.

```
